## Exact claim approvals

- **`phantom-traffic.claim.backward-pattern`** — A slow or stopped traffic pattern can move backward relative to the road while the cars travel forward. Evidence: `source.traffic.sugiyama-2008` / Article pp. 5–6; figure 4
- **`phantom-traffic.claim.vehicles`** — The reported 2008 circular-road experiment used 22 vehicles. Evidence: `source.traffic.sugiyama-2008` / Article p. 4; figure 2 caption
- **`phantom-traffic.claim.no-obstruction`** — Stop-and-go traffic can form without a physical obstruction or external bottleneck. Evidence: `source.traffic.sugiyama-2008` / Article pp. 4–5; figures 2–4
- **`phantom-traffic.claim.conditional-growth`** — In dense traffic, small speed changes can grow through following responses when the flow is unstable. Evidence: `source.traffic.treiber-2007` / Author manuscript pp. 3, 6–10; string stability, simulations and discussion
- **`phantom-traffic.claim.turnover`** — Cars enter the slow cluster at its rear and accelerate out at its front, changing the cluster’s membership. Evidence: `source.traffic.sugiyama-2008` / Article p. 5, paragraph beginning We observed the instability
- **`phantom-traffic.claim.bottleneck-distinction`** — Some congestion is generated by bottlenecks or incidents; internally generated waves are a different possibility. Evidence: `source.traffic.fhwa-2017` / Appendix A, forming and recovery wave examples

## Qualifications, exclusions and source limitations

All original qualifications remain intact: growth depends on the traffic state and following responses; density alone does not guarantee instability; response delay is not the sole cause; the backward entity is the road-relative congestion pattern; some cars may stop briefly and resume forward; neither the reported wave speed nor an experimental density is universal. The ring demonstrates possibility, not the origin of every jam. Finding an empty road after a queue can also reflect a cleared obstruction. No blame is assigned to a particular driver.

The eleven rejected formulations remain:

- One tiny brake tap always creates a traffic jam.
- A traffic jam appears from nothing.
- Drivers overreact and create all traffic jams.
- Reaction time alone causes phantom traffic jams.
- Nobody caused the traffic jam.
- All traffic jams are spontaneous backward-moving waves.
- Traffic jam waves always travel backward at 20 km/h.
- All sufficiently dense traffic must become unstable.
- Autonomous vehicles automatically eliminate congestion.
- Finding nothing ahead proves a phantom jam formed spontaneously.
- The cars in a jam drive backward.

The separate “traffic jams are waves” wording decision remains qualified: patterns/boundaries can propagate; not every jam is one spontaneously generated backward wave. The brake-tap shorthand remains replaced by the approved conditional-growth claim.

Source limitations are unchanged and hash-bound in the [original inspection record](../phantom-traffic-v1/source-inspections.json): the 2008 article and figures 2–4 were inspected; raw video/data were not independently analyzed; targeted sections rather than every appendix were inspected in the 2013 paper; the 2007 source is an author model manuscript; FHWA's bottleneck examples are scoped rather than treated as a universal denial of spontaneous jams; the inaccessible Orosz review contributes no evidence. ## Final wording analysis

Previous: **“In dense traffic, small speed changes can grow as drivers adjust to the cars ahead—if the flow is unstable.”**

Proposed: **“In dense traffic, small speed changes can sometimes grow as drivers adjust to the cars ahead.”**

The original is precise but requires the viewer to interpret the technical abstraction “unstable.” The proposed “can sometimes grow” explicitly limits the statement to possible outcomes, not all dense traffic or every disturbance. It does not assert a measured frequency, randomness or numerical probability. Stable responses can fade; amplifying responses can grow. The approved claim still specifies unstable conditions and preserves its caveats. The short omits technical classification of those conditions, rather than redefining them.

The evidence is the existing [Treiber/Kesting/Helbing manuscript](https://www.mtreiber.de/publications/timedelay_TRR07_final.pdf), pp. 3 and 7–10, particularly the contrasting stable/amplifying examples and discussion. Those sections were reopened for this wording pass. The unconditional alternative “small speed changes grow” is excluded. The example provided by the owner is now an evidence-based proposal, **not automatically approved wording**.

The cars move forward. But a traffic jam can move backward.

Researchers put twenty-two cars on a circular track. No obstruction. Yet stop-and-go traffic formed.

In dense traffic, small speed changes can sometimes grow as drivers adjust to the cars ahead.

Not every jam starts this way. But this kind needs no blocked road.
