# Wood frog freeze tolerance — Content Intelligence review

## Topic

How wood frogs survive being frozen

## Why it was selected

Selected for the first real Content Intelligence operator trial because it combines a startling biological event, a clear mechanism, strong visual potential, and authoritative source coverage.

## Claims awaiting human verification

- **SUPPORTED:** Wood frogs can survive substantial freezing of their body tissues and water during overwintering.
  Evidence: source.jeb.wood-frog-hibernation-2013 (Introduction, paragraphs describing overwintering and survival of up to two-thirds body-water freezing); source.nps.denali-wood-frog-2022 (Wood frog winter adaptation, paragraphs 2–4)
  Caveats: The amount, duration, and minimum survivable temperature vary by population and experimental conditions.
- **SUPPORTED:** During frozen dormancy, a wood frog can stop breathing and its heart can stop beating, with activity resuming after thawing.
  Evidence: source.nps.denali-wood-frog-2022 (Wood frog winter adaptation, paragraphs 3–4)
  Caveats: Before publication, a human reviewer should confirm the exact physiological wording against a primary source.
- **SUPPORTED:** Freezing triggers rapid mobilization of glucose from liver glycogen, and that glucose acts as a cryoprotectant that reduces tissue injury.
  Evidence: source.pubmed.wood-frog-glucose-1991 (Abstract); source.jeb.wood-frog-hibernation-2013 (Introduction, cryoprotectant mechanism paragraphs)
  Caveats: Glucose is one component of a broader freeze-tolerance system, not a complete explanation by itself.
- **SUPPORTED:** Urea and glucose both contribute to wood-frog cryoprotection by limiting ice formation and helping preserve cellular structures.
  Evidence: source.jeb.wood-frog-hibernation-2013 (Introduction, osmolyte and cryoprotection paragraphs); source.plos.wood-frog-cryoprotectants-2015 (Introduction, paragraphs 2–4)
  Caveats: Relative contributions differ across tissues, populations, acclimatization, and freezing history.
- **SUPPORTED:** Water redistribution and sequestration of ice in body compartments help limit damaging ice formation within organs and cells.
  Evidence: source.jeb.wood-frog-hibernation-2013 (Introduction, water redistribution and ice sequestration paragraph)
  Caveats: The popular phrase “frozen solid” must not imply that every cell interior freezes uniformly.
- **SUPPORTED:** In one laboratory study, Interior Alaskan wood frogs survived experimental freezing to temperatures as low as −16°C.
  Evidence: source.jeb.wood-frog-hibernation-2013 (Summary and Results)
  Caveats: This is population- and experiment-specific and must not be presented as a universal species threshold.
- **SUPPORTED:** In the same laboratory study, Interior Alaskan wood frogs endured a two-month freezing bout at −4°C.
  Evidence: source.jeb.wood-frog-hibernation-2013 (Summary)
  Caveats: The quantity does not establish the normal duration for every wild wood frog.

No claim is marked VERIFIED. SUPPORTED means the retrieved evidence aligns with the claim, but an owner/editor has not completed the human verification gate.

## Script draft

- This frog can freeze until its heart stops—then thaw and move again.
  Claims: wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.freeze-survival, wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.heartbeat-breathing-stop
- In winter, a wood frog can stop breathing as ice spreads through much of its body.
  Claims: wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.freeze-survival, wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.heartbeat-breathing-stop
- The trick is not stopping every crystal. Water is redirected so more ice forms in compartments where it does less damage.
  Claims: wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.ice-redistribution
- At the same time, its liver releases a surge of glucose, while urea adds another layer of protection.
  Claims: wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.glucose-cryoprotectant, wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.urea-and-glucose
- Together, those defenses limit ice formation and help protect membranes and other cell structures.
  Claims: wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.urea-and-glucose, wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.ice-redistribution
- When spring warms the frog, its heart and breathing return. It did not defeat freezing—it controlled where the damage could happen.
  Claims: wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.heartbeat-breathing-stop, wood-frog-freeze-tolerance.claim.ice-redistribution

## Narrative structure

- **Impossible opening:** Create immediate disbelief with a reversible biological flatline.
- **The freeze:** Establish that this is real body freezing rather than ordinary hibernation.
- **Put ice in safer places:** Replace the misleading frozen-solid mental model with spatially managed ice.
- **Protect the cells:** Reveal glucose and urea as complementary cryoprotective defenses.
- **Controlled, not invincible:** Resolve the apparent resurrection and restate the deeper idea.
