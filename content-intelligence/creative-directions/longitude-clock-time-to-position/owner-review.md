## Exact approved narration

How can a clock tell a ship where it is?

By keeping the time at a known reference point.

Sailors use the Sun to find local time, corrected to match the clock’s timekeeping.

Compare them at the same moment.

Earth’s turning links that time difference to longitude: fifteen degrees per hour.

Twelve at the reference. Two in the afternoon locally.

Two hours ahead. Thirty degrees east.

Harrison’s H4 helped demonstrate accurate timekeeping at sea.

Other astronomical methods remained useful too.

The clock carries time. Observation and calculation reveal longitude.

89 words; approximately 35.6–38.1 seconds spoken at 140–150 words/minute, 41–47 seconds with 5–8 seconds of intentional breathing. These are estimates, not generated/measured audio.

## Proposed execution

**visual-medium:** Original authored 2.5D crafted object theatre: shallow-relief illustrated objects on one navigational workbench. No photoreal generated footage or stock montage. A consistent physical metaphor connects a carried clock to an abstract angular result without changing worlds.

**art-direction:** A modern museum demonstration rather than sepia period drama: precise objects, generous negative space, visible analytical relationships. Objects give maritime history weight while declared abstraction prevents false historical footage.

**realism:** Recognizable silhouettes and restrained material depth; schematic ocean, hull, Sun and globe. Orthographic/planar geometry for the proof. Enough identity for context; less realism avoids implying an actual historical crew, voyage or exact observational procedure.

**historical-reconstruction:** Only the brief H4 study needs historical specificity. No named ship, costume, room, trial reading or fictitious captain; explanatory ship glyph is explicitly non-literal. The mechanism is the story. Extra eighteenth-century staging adds unapproved assertions without payoff.

**ocean-navigation:** Shallow-relief water and a small abstract sailing hull establish at-sea context; calm surface, no disaster/storm spectacle. Navigation context matters; movement must not be mistaken for elapsed-voyage distance.

**reference-location:** A chosen meridian on the same globe remains fixed and tethered to REFERENCE TIME. Do not automatically label it Greenwich. The reference is east-west angular origin; historical navigation did not universally use modern Greenwich.

**solar-observation:** Sun/horizon glyph produces observed local solar time; a visibly separate correction card produces LOCAL MEAN TIME. Reference object remains untouched. This expresses observation plus astronomical correction without a technical derivation or false clock-reset implication.

**simultaneous-time:** Both readouts coexist, freeze together and carry SAME INSTANT. BOTH MEAN SOLAR TIME appears before comparison and remains through numerical proof. Simultaneity and comparable basis are causal conditions, not footnotes to hide.

**clock-watch:** Reference-time object carries hours only. Generic explanatory readout cards use 12:00 and 14:00; no geographic digits on a clock face. A timekeeper preserves reference time; local time is independently obtained. 24-hour explanatory notation is not historical dial design.

**earth-longitude:** One small globe transitions through object rotation to a north-pole plan view of the globe with meridian-ray proof with fixed reference and explicit east direction. No geographical pinpoint. Longitude is angular offset. Flat proof view avoids perspective distorting a 30° wedge and implies no latitude.

**time-angle-transformation:** Keep REFERENCE 12:00 / LOCAL 14:00 visible together; local minus reference reveals +2 HOURS, then a true 30° wedge east of the reference. Numbers hold, no count-up roulette. The comparison becomes the viewer’s payoff; preserve sign and units rather than merely flashing an answer.

**h-four:** Brief large watch-form original reconstruction identified H4 · EXPLANATORY RECONSTRUCTION; silver case/enamel dial and proportion from ZAA0037. No later boxed marine chronometer or invented internal mechanism. Supports the precise engineering contribution without lone-genius biography or prize/adoption myths.

**palette:** Matte warm ivory #F1EBDD, deep aubergine #322D3A for text, muted terracotta #9D493E for the derived angular result and desaturated sea-green #526C68 for local observation. Warm object-study contrast separates this world from the historical void/blue/gold palette. Labels/shapes must convey roles without relying on color.

**lighting:** Soft broad daylight across relief, minimal cast shadows. Flatten lighting under the paired readings and meridian proof. Material depth creates beauty; analytical geometry must remain unambiguous and high contrast.

**materials:** Paper-like matte diagram surface, ceramic/enamel face, restrained silver watch rim and shallow water relief. No photographic grunge or fake archival film. Texture suggests careful instrumentation, but text/measurement planes stay clean.

Avoid antique script and existing oversized all-caps display stacks. Serif gives an object-study character; humanist sans favors reading the correction and numerical proof. Fonts need later integration, licensed files and fit tests.

**information-graphics:** Paired labeled cards and one meridian arc; reference/local role positions persist. Minimal secondary text. Mean-time and simultaneity safeguards take priority over decorative labels. A stable low-load information hierarchy explains the chain on a phone.

**captions:** Adaptive V2, full narration-faithful phrase coverage with one or two speech-led lines in Source Sans 3. Dark aubergine on an intentional quiet ivory field; emphasis by weight rather than ice/gold color. Separate scene labels are explanatory, not substitutes for spoken coverage.
