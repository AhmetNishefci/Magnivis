# AHMET — PREMISE REVIEW

Cycle #10 independently selected one opportunity after fresh discovery and two preserved comparison phases within one three-round budget. This is the publishing opportunity for approval, not a script or production proposal.

**PLANNED VIDEO**

How did an enslaved couple escape openly—with the wife posing as a slaveholder and her husband as her slave?

**WHY A COLD VIEWER MAY CARE**

Ellen and William Craft’s freedom depended on strangers accepting the very relationship trapping them. A costume seems fragile: what happens when someone expects her to speak, sign her name or prove she owns him?

**EXPECTED PAYOFF**

Understand why apparent illness was essential to the plan: it explained the inability to sign, reduced dangerous conversation and encouraged deference. Then understand why an ownership checkpoint could still nearly defeat them. The disguise reveal opens the explanation; it does not exhaust it.

**TRUTH / EVIDENCE STATUS**

Preliminary historical reconstruction. Relevant passages of their 1860 participant account, a footnoted National Park Service history and specialist scholarship were inspected. A near-contemporary letter supports a further evidence route, but only its modern transcription was inspected. Later accounts often share the memoir as their source; exact dialogue and checkpoint details still require full review. Reaching the North did not mean permanent safety.

**INTENDED STORY**

Follow the journey through the problems their assumed identities created, how apparent illness helped, and the checkpoint it could not simply answer. Resolve the escape without implying permanent freedom on arrival. No final narrative or visual treatment has been developed.

**WHY MAGNIVIS SELECTED IT**

Immediate human stakes combine with an ingenious, understandable plan. Knowing “she used a disguise” leaves substantial questions about how it survived scrutiny. Serious rivals and evidence-access limits are preserved internally; neither Cycle #9 similarity nor domain rotation supplied the choice.

**MATERIAL RISK**

Preserve both people’s agency and the stakes of enslavement. The story must not become costume comedy or invented dialogue. If fuller evidence undermines the promised explanation, return to discovery and a new Premise Review.
