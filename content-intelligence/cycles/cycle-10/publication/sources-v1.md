# Cycle10 source guide

The verified package remains revision-v2; V3 changed presentation only.

- **Later participant account:** William Craft, *Running a Thousand Miles for Freedom* (1860). Detailed disguise, conversation, Baltimore demand and discretionary release are attributed to this later account, not contemporaneously verified scenes. https://docsouth.unc.edu/neh/craft/craft.html
- **Near-contemporary report:** William Wells Brown’s January4,1849 letter, printed in *The Liberator*, January12,p3. Brown met the Crafts after the escape; he did not witness their journey. Supports the injured-hand/signature explanation and strategy. https://fair-use.org/the-liberator/1849/01/12/the-liberator-19-02.pdf
- **Independent contemporary evidence of later danger:** *The Liberator*, November1,1850,p2, documents the Boston recapture attempt. https://fair-use.org/the-liberator/1850/11/01/the-liberator-20-44.pdf
- **Institutional synthesis:** National Park Service escape history; does not independently corroborate each memoir detail. https://www.nps.gov/articles/-a-desperate-leap-for-liberty-the-escape-of-william-and-ellen-craft.htm
- **Scholarly inspection:** Martinez (2023), *Journal of American Studies*57(5),637–676, DOI10.1017/S0021875823000567, includes a near-contemporary traveler facsimile. Copied Brown material is not additional independent corroboration. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0021875823000567

Original inspected source bytes, claim-level evidence, exclusions and rights remain in ../research and ../revision-v2/knowledge-package.ready.json. No invented dialogue, inner thoughts, escape footage or recreated original documents. Archival portraits are later publications; document diagrams are explanatory illustrations.
