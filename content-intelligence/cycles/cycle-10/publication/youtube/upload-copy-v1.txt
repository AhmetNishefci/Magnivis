TITLE
How an Enslaved Couple Escaped in Plain Sight

DESCRIPTION
In 1848, Ellen and William Craft escaped slavery by traveling openly. Ellen posed as a white male slaveholder; William posed as her enslaved servant. But the identity that opened doors created new vulnerabilities: signing a register, answering questions, and being asked for assurance of ownership.

Detailed Baltimore checkpoint events are reported in William Craft’s later 1860 account. A report published weeks after the escape supports the bound-hand/signature explanation; it was not an eyewitness account of the journey.

Reaching Philadelphia did not guarantee lasting safety. After men came to Boston to recapture them in 1850, the Crafts left for England. Their ingenuity was a joint strategy under the stakes of enslavement.

Archival portraits and original explanatory graphics; no escape footage. AI-generated narration.
