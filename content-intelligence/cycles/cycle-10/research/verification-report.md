It establishes early reporting of the married couple’s role inversion, Ellen’s inability to write, her tied right hand to avoid registers, and arrival after four days. Brown met the Crafts shortly afterward; this is near-contemporary reporting, probably based on their information, not a journey eyewitness.

It describes the apparent invalid’s facial bandage, spectacles, support by the servant and the servant’s illness explanation. Identification as the Crafts followed reading Brown’s report; that retrospective identification limits independence. Its appended Brown extract counts once. Its perception of Spanish extraction prevents a universal claim about observers’ racial interpretation. No peripheral literary interpretation becomes a historical action claim.

This is later testimony, not a contemporaneous checkpoint record. The script explicitly says “in their account” and “William’s account says.” No exact dialogue, fabricated countdown, thoughts, emotions or guaranteed causal mechanism is used. The conductor’s confirmation was evidence of prior travel, not proof of ownership. The Charleston episode is retained in research but omitted from narration because the Baltimore test contributes stronger additional understanding without redundant checkpoint buildup.
