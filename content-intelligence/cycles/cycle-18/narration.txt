Tesla showed a Roadster driving upside down, then briefly airborne.

But those are two different engineering tricks.

Tesla says fans remove air from beneath the car.

Lower pressure underneath lets outside air press it against the surface.

Upside down, that force must beat gravity.

Tesla attributes the airborne motion to cold-gas thrusters.

They release compressed air. Gas shoots one way; the car is pushed the other.

Different jet directions can change its motion. Off-center pushes can turn it.

But the stored gas is limited.

A short, controlled maneuver is not proof of sustained, practical flight.
