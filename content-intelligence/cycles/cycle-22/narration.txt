Some fish physically fuse with their mates.

In the deep sea, a tiny male searches for a much larger female.

This is the triplewart seadevil.

He grips her skin with hooked teeth. In this species, attachment becomes permanent.

Over time, their tissues join. Blood vessels connect.

Her blood supplies his nutrients, while his testes develop to produce sperm.

He doesn't disappear into her. He remains an attached male.

How can foreign tissues coexist? These fish have lost key parts of adaptive immunity.

Exactly how their remaining defenses work is still being studied.

In a world where mates are hard to find, staying together may secure future spawning.

Two fish. A lasting connection.
