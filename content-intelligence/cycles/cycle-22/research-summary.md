# Cycle 22 — scientific evidence and inspection limits

Selected animal: the triplewart seadevil, **Cryptopsaras couesii** (older literature also uses *C. couesi*). This species has permanently parasitic males. The original female has a short lure, three dorsal caruncles and spinulose skin; the tiny male has prominent eyes and no female lure. Juvenile reference proportions are not treated as adult measurements.

Ceratioids include temporary attachment, facultative permanent attachment and obligate permanent attachment. These categories do not apply uniformly to all anglerfish. The film restricts its mechanism to the selected permanently attaching species. Pietsch's 2005 comparative review supports the distinctions; its numerical species counts are historical. Reports of nonfused *Melanocetus johnsonii* attachments are retained in reserve evidence, not substituted for *Cryptopsaras* anatomy or treated as a complete observed spawning sequence.

Pietsch's 1975 species-specific study describes hooked jaw denticles, apparent reliance on vision, epidermal union followed by circulatory connection and nutritional dependence. The 1976 primary paper was inspected in full from a third-party scan, including anatomical text and the juvenile-pair image. That paper documents retained heart/gill structures and variable reductions; respiratory pathways and the details of degeneration are not fully resolved. The narration therefore makes no claim that every organ disappears, that the male loses all identity, or that female blood is his only oxygen source. Some free-living males already lack functional feeding: fusion is not asserted to cause the initial loss of feeding.

Permanent tissue and vascular connections allow female-supplied nutrients and the male's continued reproductive role. Testicular development and apparent repeated spawning are supported by Pietsch's primary/review evidence and the expert Tree of Life account. Sperm is never illustrated travelling through the shared blood. The film does not invent fertilization timing, a precise spawning trigger, a complete gonadal map, fusion duration or measured blood-vessel topology.

Swann et al. (2020, Science) link attachment strategies to different losses of adaptive immune functions. Brownstein et al. (2024, Current Biology) report convergent immune degeneration within an evolutionary analysis. Neither licenses the claim that every species has no immune system, that the bite switches immunity off, or that the cellular mechanism of tolerance is completely solved. A June 2026 Max Planck project announcement describes ongoing investigation of remaining defenses; it is a prospective research announcement, not a new experimental result. The film calls the remaining defenses an open question.

Satow and Takimoto (2026, Proceedings B) provide a mathematical model supporting reproductive assurance when encounters are scarce and costs low. This is a proposed evolutionary explanation, not direct observation of historical selection or an intentional purpose. The narration uses **may**.
