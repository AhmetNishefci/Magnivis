## Locked narration — unchanged

A Venus flytrap has no brain. Yet a touch can leave a fading memory.

A brief bend of a sensitive hair can send an electrical signal through the trap while it stays open.

Alongside that signal, calcium rises inside the leaf’s cells.

Then the calcium level falls. For a short time, part of that change remains.

Another signal arriving soon enough raises the level further. If it reaches the level needed for closure, the trap can snap shut.

Wait too long, and the next signal may no longer reach that level.

Two signals don’t always mean two touches: one slower hair movement can produce both.

Here, memory means retained information—a fading chemical state, not conscious recollection.
