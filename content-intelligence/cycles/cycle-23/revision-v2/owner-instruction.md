## Creative assessment

V1 has a compelling story and coherent chronology, but the repeated static portraits and workplace compositions reduce its cinematic impact.

The goal is NOT to add constant camera movement to still photographs.

The goal is to show consequential visual changes that advance the narrative.

## Required improvements

### 1. Opening: 0–9.5 seconds

Preserve the powerful spoken hook.

Create a more compelling opening progression. Establish the engineer, the two cities and the extraordinary nature of the story without prematurely revealing every event.

Avoid holding essentially the same portrait throughout the introduction.

### 2. Hiroshima: 9.5–20.13 seconds

Make the transition from ordinary working life to the bombing aftermath clearer.

Use restrained environmental changes, perspective shifts and carefully staged consequences.

Do not add graphic injury, invented explosions or misleading historical footage.

### 3. The journey: 20.13–27.50 seconds

Make this sequence a centerpiece.

Improve the visual relationship between Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

Combine geographic clarity with a convincing sense of travel and passage of time.

The audience must understand that he departed Hiroshima on August 7 and arrived in Nagasaki on August 8.

Do not invent a precise railway route.

### 4. Nagasaki: 27.50–38.37 seconds

This is the narrative climax.

Reduce reliance on another nearly identical seated portrait.

Establish the workplace through varied, coherent shots.

Build a clear visual transition from the recalled conversation to the sudden second flash.

Use restrained sound and light design rather than sensational explosions.

### 5. Aftermath: 38.37–47.53 seconds

Communicate survival and the broader human cost without returning to the same portrait composition.

Preserve the distinction that his wife and infant son survived the Nagasaki bombing.

Avoid invented family reunions or undocumented personal experiences.

### 6. Ending: 47.53–64.37 seconds

The recognition map communicates the facts but should not feel visually repetitive.

Make the final transition toward his later-life advocacy more emotionally resonant.

Keep the conclusion dignified, reflective and historically grounded.
