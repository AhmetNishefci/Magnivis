The central explanation survives only in qualified form: movement-related prediction contributes to attenuation of some self-produced touch. It is an important clue to the everyday self-tickle contrast. It is not a settled complete explanation of laughter-inducing tickling. This distinction is spoken in the master, rather than confined to these notes.

Competing/complementary explanations considered: movement gating; peripheral self-touch feedback and broad coincident suppression; attentional/precision accounts; social and affective context. This short neither selects one complete mechanism of gargalesis nor implies unexpected external contact is sufficient for laughter. Anticipation of another person's tickle is not equivalent to motor-related prediction of one's own touch. No clinical subgroup, diagnosis, treatment, protective evolutionary purpose, universal tickle threshold or specific brain-region causal claim is made.
