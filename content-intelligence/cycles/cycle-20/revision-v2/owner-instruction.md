## Creative assessment

V1 is scientifically careful and technically validated, but the visual presentation does not yet meet our intended premium cinematic standard.

Specific concerns:

1. The opening uses a simplified mannequin on a light background. It communicates the situation but lacks a strong visual hook.
2. The prediction sequence relies too much on a static explanatory diagram.
3. The overall visual palette and composition remain too uniform.
4. Several explanatory labels are small and less effective on mobile.
5. The ending explains the idea but could provide a stronger visual payoff.

These are creative-direction issues, not established factual defects.

## V2 objectives

### 1. Stronger opening — 0–4 seconds

Make the contrast between another person's touch and self-generated touch visually immediate.

Use compelling close-up framing, tactile motion, expressive physical reactions, dynamic camera work, and polished lighting.

Avoid exaggerated or medically unsupported reactions.

The first two seconds should create curiosity even with sound muted.

### 2. Cinematic prediction mechanism

Replace or substantially enhance the static prediction diagram with a dynamic, visually intuitive sequence.

Show intended movement, anticipated contact, actual contact, and the resulting weaker sensation through clearly differentiated visual states.

The viewer should understand the causal mechanism without needing to read several small labels.

Use visual metaphor responsibly. Do not imply that hypothetical signal animations are literal observed neural recordings.

### 3. Meaningful visual development

Improve pacing through purposeful camera movement, scale changes, transitions, and contrast.

Avoid constant movement merely for decoration.

Preserve the experimentally important robotic-delay sequence, making the difference between immediate and delayed touch unmistakable.

### 4. Stronger final payoff

Return to the original self-touch experiment.

This time, make the viewer visually recognize the prediction happening before contact.

The ending should feel like the resolution of the opening mystery, not another explanatory slide.

### 5. Mobile-first polish

Prioritize readable main captions, large meaningful subjects, uncluttered frames, safe-area awareness, and coherent visual hierarchy.

Small scientific qualifications may remain where necessary, but avoid relying on tiny text for the core explanation.
