Why can their fingers make you laugh, but yours usually can't?

Both touch your skin. One important clue is prediction.

When you move your hand, your nervous system can anticipate the touch it will produce.

That touch can feel weaker. Scientists call this sensory attenuation.

In experiments, self-produced touch felt less ticklish than matching external touch.

Adding a delay to a robotic self-touch made it feel more ticklish.

But those experiments used light touch, not full-on tickle laughter.

You can give yourself a light, tickly sensation. Laughter-inducing tickling is harder to explain, and people differ.

Prediction is an important clue, not the whole answer.

Your nervous system doesn't just receive touch. It can anticipate your own.
