His Defense: The Vermeer Was His Own Forgery

POST CAPTION
A supposed Vermeer reached Hermann Göring through an art dealer. Han van Meegeren’s defense: he had painted it himself.

Investigators examined materials and crack patterns. Old supports concealed modern resin-based paint. The evidence supported his confession, but did not make him innocent: in 1947 he was convicted of fraud and forgery and sentenced to one year.

Emmaus, shown here as an example of earlier expert acceptance, was not the painting sold to Göring.

Sources: Froentjes & de Wild, court examiners’ report (1949); Metropolitan Museum of Art, Art Forgery (1968); contemporary TIME trial report: https://time.com/archive/6823954/art-truth-consequences/

Archival photographs: Bram Wisman and Koos Raucamp / Anefo, Nationaal Archief (CC0).
