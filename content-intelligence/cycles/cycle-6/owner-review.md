## Exact final narration

In 1834, Britain’s Parliament caught fire because workers were destroying old receipts.

The receipts were wooden sticks.

For centuries, the government recorded payments with notches, then split the wood so both sides kept a matching record.

When that system became obsolete, two cartloads of sticks were left to dispose of.

Workers fed them into heating furnaces beneath the House of Lords.

A chimney fire smouldered under the chamber floor. Heat and smoke were noticed, but the burning continued.

That evening, flames broke through.

Both parliamentary chambers were destroyed. Westminster Hall survived.

The dangerous part wasn’t keeping records on wood. It was choosing where to burn them.
