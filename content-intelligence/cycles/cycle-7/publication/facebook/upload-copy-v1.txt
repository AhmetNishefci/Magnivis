An Obstacle Detected—Without Absorbing the Photon

POST CAPTION
How can light reveal an obstacle without being absorbed or reflected by it?

With two possible paths available, quantum interference keeps one detector dark. An absorbing obstacle blocks a path and removes that cancellation.

Some trials absorb the photon. But in a successful trial, the formerly dark detector receives it. The obstacle is detected without absorbing that photon.

This is interaction-free measurement: a probabilistic result, not a guarantee of zero interaction across all trials. Other trials absorb the photon or give no clear answer.

Experimental account: Kwiat, Weinfurter & Zeilinger, Quantum Seeing in the Dark (1996).
