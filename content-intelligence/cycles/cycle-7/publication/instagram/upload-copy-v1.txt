POST CAPTION
How can light reveal an obstacle without being absorbed or reflected by it?

The clue is a detector that stays dark when both possible paths are available. Block one path, and the cancellation keeping it dark is removed.

In some trials, that detector clicks. The photon reached the detector, so the obstacle did not absorb it—yet the click reveals the obstacle.

Interaction-free measurement is probabilistic. Other trials absorb the photon or give no clear answer. The two paths are quantum possibilities, not two half-photons.
