## Exact narration

How can light reveal an obstacle without being absorbed or reflected by it? Send one photon into an interferometer: two possible paths, joined again. With both paths open and the device correctly aligned, interference keeps one detector dark. The waves associated with the two paths cancel at that output. Now put a light-absorbing object in one path. If it absorbs the photon, neither output detector clicks. But sometimes the formerly dark detector clicks instead. That single photon reached the detector, so the object did not absorb it. The blocked path removed the cancellation. The click reveals the obstacle. Other trials absorb the photon or give no clear answer. This is not a guaranteed harmless test. Researchers have demonstrated this effect, called interaction-free measurement. The clue comes from changing which paths are available.
