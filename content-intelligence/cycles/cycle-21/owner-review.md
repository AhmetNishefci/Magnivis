## Final narration

What if an animal could reverse its own life cycle?

This is Turritopsis dohrnii.

Normally, attached polyps bud off tiny jellyfish, which grow into swimming adults.

But injury or starvation can send an adult another way.

Its bell collapses. Its tentacles disappear. It settles into a cyst-like mass.

Inside, cells reorganize. Some can change their specialized identity: transdifferentiation.

A stolon grows, then polyps. That colony can bud new jellyfish.

Life-cycle reversal can happen repeatedly in laboratory cultures.

The molecular controls are still being studied.

But predators can still kill it. Reversal isn't guaranteed survival.

It doesn't make death impossible. It makes the direction of development reversible.

- Normal life cycle includes gametes→planula→attached polyp colony→budding medusae→mature medusae. The short narrates the polyp-to-medusa leg. Matsumoto/Piraino/Miglietta2019 G3, DOI10.1534/g3.119.400487; Smithsonian NEMESIS species account.
- Damage and starvation can trigger reversal; laboratory induction can also use CsCl. Not every animal survives every attempt. No implication that normal aging is a literal rewind. NHM institutional account;2019 G3 and Pascual-Torner etal2022 PNAS, DOI10.1073/pnas.2118763119.
- Supported route: medusa regression/loss of recognizable structures→attached cyst-like stage→stolon→polyps/colony→budding new medusae. Actual elapsed time varies and is compressed. PNAS2022 Fig1/methods; G3 2019 stages.
- Tissue excision and development support differentiated-cell participation/transdifferentiation. No exhaustive single-cell fate map, all-cell survival or exact observed molecular event is shown. Piraino etal1996, DOI10.2307/1543022 (abstract inspected; historical Mediterranean T.nutricula naming); modern G3 2019 discussion. Expression/genomic pathways remain proposed explanations, not proven reversal switches: GBE2021 evab136 and PNAS2022.
- Repeated reversal is laboratory scoped. Hasegawa etal2023 DNAResearch dsac047 publisher-indexed introduction was inspected; full article open was blocked. No numerical count is asserted for identified T.dohrnii. Kubota2011 ten reversals concerned unidentified Turritopsis sp., excluded from narration.
- Predators can kill it; reversal does not guarantee survival or establish infection immunity. Specific disease incidence and natural longevity were not established; no quantitative disease claim is made. NHM account; biological transformation is not universal invulnerability.
- Species are not interchangeable. T.rubra lacks published postreproductive rejuvenation in the2022 comparison; this does not establish inability at every developmental stage. Historical Mediterranean nutricula terminology does not generalize to all current nutricula populations.
