## Creative assessment

The scientific narrative is strong, the life-cycle stages are correctly distinguished, and the existing narration should be preserved.

However, the visual presentation is too diagrammatic for this subject. The jellyfish is relatively small, the underwater setting changes little, and the central transformation lacks the visual richness and sense of discovery expected from Magnivis.

We want viewers to feel that they are witnessing an extraordinary biological event, not simply watching a labeled life-cycle chart.

## Required V2 improvements

### 1. Opening: 0–6 seconds

Make the jellyfish the unmistakable visual hero.

Use a closer, more detailed view with convincing translucency, internal anatomy, tentacle motion, lighting, depth, and subtle underwater movement.

The opening should create visual curiosity immediately, even without audio.

Preserve anatomical credibility for Turritopsis dohrnii. Do not substitute an unrelated species.

### 2. Transformation: approximately 12–34 seconds

This is the highest-priority revision.

Make the medusa-to-cyst-to-stolon-to-polyp sequence visually continuous and compelling.

Show the adult's recognizable structures progressively regressing, the cyst-like stage forming, the stolon extending, and polyps developing.

Use deliberate close-ups and transitions so viewers understand that the stages belong to the same developmental process.

Avoid depicting literal time reversal or an adult shrinking directly into a baby medusa.

### 3. Cellular reorganization

Replace the regular floating-cell grid with a more organic, spatially coherent visualization.

Make it feel like a close examination of biological tissue undergoing reorganization, not independent decorative particles.

Maintain the distinction between scientifically supported cellular participation and illustrative, unobserved microscopic details.

### 4. Cinematic development

Increase meaningful visual variety through:

- Controlled macro camera movement and depth changes.
- Better translucent materials and lighting.
- Environmental depth and underwater atmosphere.
- Distinct compositions for each developmental stage.
- Smooth visual continuity and recognizable subject identity.

Do not introduce excessive effects, random particles, or generic stock footage.

### 5. Ending: approximately 47–53 seconds

Deliver a visually satisfying payoff.

Return to the new medusa emerging from the polyp colony, linking the final image clearly to the original adult and the completed developmental reversal.

The ending should communicate wonder while retaining the important qualification that reversal does not guarantee survival.
