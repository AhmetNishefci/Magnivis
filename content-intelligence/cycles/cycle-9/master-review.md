## Exact narration

Barbados scored an own goal on purpose. Then they defended both goals. It was a Caribbean Cup qualifier in 1994. And winning the match wasn't enough. Barbados needed to win by two goals to qualify. Late in the game, they led two-one. A win—but still elimination. They could try to score again. Or exploit an unusual rule. A draw at full time meant extra time. The first goal there would end the match—and count double. So, one goal in extra time could give Barbados the two-goal margin they needed. They deliberately scored into their own net. Two-two. Now they just needed to keep it tied. But Grenada had spotted a way to stop them. Score against Barbados, and Grenada wins. Score into their own goal, and Grenada loses by only one—still enough to qualify. Before full time, either goal would do. Barbados had to protect their own goal—and Grenada's. The opponent's net had become something they needed to defend. They kept it two-two until full time. Then Barbados scored the extra-time winner. It counted as a two-goal victory. They qualified. Scoring against themselves hadn't been the mistake. It was their way of making the rules work for them.
