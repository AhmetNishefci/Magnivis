Over fifteen billion miles away, Voyager One was sending signals nobody could read.

The trouble began in November twenty twenty-three: a failed memory chip in its data-packaging computer.

Engineers couldn't repair the chip. So they moved the affected code.

But it wouldn't fit in one place.

They split it across working memory, then updated the instructions so the pieces still worked together.

A command took about twenty-two and a half hours just to reach the spacecraft.

On April twentieth, twenty twenty-four, readable engineering data came back. Science data followed later.

The chip stayed broken. The software worked around it.
