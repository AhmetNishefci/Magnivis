Topic: Why You Never See Your Eyes Move in a Mirror

Owner decision:
Proceed with Cycle #19 as the next Magnivis short-form video.

Creative objective:
Create a visually compelling explanation of why people cannot ordinarily observe their own rapid eye movements while looking directly into a mirror.

Working hook:
"Try looking from one eye to the other in a mirror. Why can't you catch your eyes moving?"

Production instructions:

1. Read AGENTS.md and all relevant canonical repository documentation. Inspect current project state and preserve previous cycles.
2. Research saccades, saccadic suppression, visual stability, and the mirror-eye phenomenon using credible neuroscience and vision-science sources.
3. Establish the precise mechanism before locking the script. Distinguish reduced visual sensitivity during saccades from other factors involved in perceiving one's own eye movements. Avoid oversimplified claims that the brain goes blind or deletes time.
4. Develop a clear 35–50-second narrative with a surprising opening, an understandable visual demonstration, and a satisfying scientific explanation.
5. Use coherent cinematic animation: a person facing a mirror, close-ups of the eyes, a first-person viewpoint, and a carefully labeled comparison with an external camera.
6. Prioritize continuous visual storytelling over generic stock footage, disconnected illustrations, or repetitive shots.
7. Ensure the mirror demonstration is accurate. Do not imply that a recorded camera view and direct mirror perception are equivalent.
8. Produce one polished V1 master with narration, sound design, and legible burned-in captions.
9. Follow existing research, licensing, production, QA, artifact-integrity, and documentation conventions.
10. Run relevant tests and report limitations transparently.
