## Exact narration —116 words

Look from one eye to the other in a mirror. Why can't you normally catch the movement?

Now record your eyes, and watch it back. There it is.

Each quick jump is a saccade.

In the mirror, the eyes you're watching are also the eyes doing the looking.

As they turn, the image sweeps across the retina.

Visual sensitivity drops around the movement. That's saccadic suppression.

The clear views before and after can also mask the brief blur.

You aren't completely blind. These effects help keep vision stable.

Watching a recording later separates the movement from your own eye turn.

The mirror never stopped showing it. Your visual system normally keeps the sweep out of awareness.
