## Exact preserved script

Scientists deliberately tipped this floating laboratory upright. Why?

Because when your instrument moves, it’s harder to tell what the ocean is doing.

FLIP was built to study underwater sound. Even a research submarine turned enough to limit those measurements.

So engineers made a laboratory that travelled horizontally, then flooded ballast tanks to lower its stern.

The lab stayed above water, while about ninety metres of hull extended beneath it.

Look at the shape: a narrow section near the surface, with a broader hull deep below.

In deep water, the motion caused by surface waves weakens with depth.

FLIP’s shape made it much less responsive to those waves.

It could still drift and tilt. Stable didn’t mean motionless.

And when the work was done, compressed air pushed water out of the ballast tanks, returning it to horizontal.

They tipped the laboratory upright to make a moving ocean easier to measure.
