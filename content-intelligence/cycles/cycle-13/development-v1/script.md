# Cycle #13 — internally reviewed script candidate

An earthquake nearly four thousand miles away made waves in this Nevada desert pool.

On July twenty-ninth, twenty twenty-five, a magnitude eight point eight quake struck off Russia’s Kamchatka Peninsula.

Twenty-one minutes later, water in Devils Hole began sloshing.

But seawater hadn’t crossed the ocean into Nevada.

Seismic waves traveled through the ground.

As the ground moved, water already inside the cave rocked back and forth—like water in a shaken bathtub.

That oscillation is called a seismic seiche, different from an ocean tsunami.

The waves here were about ten inches.

Small, but enough to disturb a shallow shelf where the endangered Devils Hole pupfish feed and spawn.

This event removed some material from the shelf. The park described it as a minor disturbance.

An isolated-looking pool can reveal just how connected the ground beneath us really is.
